Workload Venue Strategy

Choose where a workload belongs from the business requirement, then test the economics, operating constraints and ability to recover.

Public guidance. No contact details are needed to read this guide. Use it to frame a decision, then validate the requirements of your own environment.

Begin with the decision

A useful venue review ends with a decision someone can own: retain the present arrangement, improve it, move a defined workload, or defer a move until a dependency is resolved. It should explain why that decision supports the business, what it costs to act on it and what evidence would cause the decision to change.

Start with one workload or a small, related group. Agree the service owner, the users affected, the required availability, the decision date and the problem that triggered the review. Avoid treating the whole estate as one decision. A customer-facing transaction, an archive and an experimental AI service may have very different needs.

Build a usable baseline

Record the current platform, location, integrations, data classes, usage pattern, service requirements and operating owner. Include backups, recovery dependencies and the people needed to support the workload. Distinguish what is measured from what is assumed. Missing information is a discovery task, not a reason to choose the most familiar provider.

Reconcile cost to the service being delivered. A provider invoice alone may omit licensing, network traffic, operational support, monitoring, backup, resilience and internal effort. Use the same scope and period when comparing venues. Document assumptions so a reviewer can challenge them without rebuilding the entire analysis.

Compare the options on the same terms

Consider retaining the current arrangement as a genuine option. Compare public cloud, customer-owned infrastructure, hosted private capacity and hybrid arrangements only where they can meet the workload requirements. Define any mandatory constraints first. An option that fails a required recovery or control condition should not win because its headline price is lower.

Ask how each option behaves under normal demand, peak demand and a plausible failure. Identify which costs change with usage and which commitments remain if demand falls. Include the one-off work needed to migrate, integrate, test and train. The result should show trade-offs rather than hide them behind a single unexplained ranking.

Check whether the move can be operated

A technically available venue may still be a poor operating choice. Confirm who monitors the service, responds to incidents, approves changes and controls access. Establish whether the required skills are available internally or through a named partner, and who remains accountable when several suppliers are involved.

Test exit and recovery assumptions with representative evidence. A contract permitting export does not demonstrate that the data can be restored and used elsewhere. Agree what must be recoverable, in what form, with which dependencies and under whose control. Use qualified specialists where contractual, security or jurisdictional questions require them.

Turn the recommendation into an executable decision

Prepare a short decision record: the business outcome, the baseline, feasible options, key assumptions, recommended path, unresolved questions, costs, risks and accountable approver. Separate the placement decision from the implementation plan. Approval of a preferred venue is not permission to skip migration testing or business acceptance.

Sequence the work. Resolve the most consequential unknowns first, then run a bounded validation or pilot. Define success and rollback conditions before a migration begins. Retain the previous operating position until the agreed acceptance and recovery conditions have been met.

Use this checklist in the next discussion

Can the business owner state the outcome and acceptable disruption? Is the workload inventory current? Are cost comparisons like-for-like? Have data, access, resilience and provider dependencies been checked? Is operating ownership clear? Can the proposed decision be explained without relying on a product name?

Leave the meeting with named owners for unanswered questions, the evidence each must produce and the next decision date. Revisit placement when demand, economics, controls or business priorities change. A sound venue decision can evolve without turning every review into another migration.
